## How to Use This Workbook

The vowel transcription is based on a neutral sung-English pronunciation. Individual vowels may change slightly according to accent, pitch and interpretation.

Consonants appear in lowercase. The principal sung vowels appear as capitalised codes.

When practising:

- Keep the jaw released rather than pushed downward.
- Allow the tongue and lips to form each vowel without squeezing the throat.
- Imagine one continuous stream of breath connecting the vowels.
- Do not try to hold the throat artificially wide.
- Practise softly before increasing volume or pitch.
- Stop if you experience pain, scraping, persistent hoarseness or throat pressure.

## PART ONE: VOWEL KEY

### Front Vowels

- **EE** — as in everybody, Chinese, Billy, feet, keep
- **IH** — as in little, bit, kicking, skip, and the final syllables of fighting, lightning
- **EH** — as in expert, men, said, get, let's
- **AY** — as in fighting, lightning, China, timing, made, swaying
- **AE** — as in cats, fast, fact, stand, hands

### Back and Central Vowels

- **AH** — as in Kung, was, funky, chopping, up, and the huh/ha interjections
- **UH** — as in were, expert, took, sure
- **OE** — as in the repeated *ho* syllables, plus unstressed a/the in this transcription
- **AW** — as in fought, down, bow, now, Chinatown, boss
- **OO** — as in Fu, knew, new, you, into
- **OW** — as in oh, and the opening of the repeated vocal refrain

### Transcription notes

- Unstressed *a*/*the* appear as **OE** in this breakdown. At tempo they are a
  quick, neutral schwa — do **not** round them into a full "oh"; they should
  cost nothing.
- *were/expert/sure* use **UH** followed by r — a relaxed central vowel with a
  light r-colour, not a hard curled ER.
- The **-ing** family (*fighting, lightning, frightening, timing*) always ends
  **AY → IH-ng**: the bright AY carries the note; the IH-ng is a light tail.
  This rhyme family is the engine of the whole chorus.

## PART THREE: VOWEL PRACTICE SETS

*Same legato method as the Farnham workbooks — one ribbon of breath, vowels
merging, consonants added only after the vowel line is seamless. This song
adds a second dimension the belters didn't have: **groove**. The later sets
practise placing those legato vowels precisely on the beat, because in this
song the rhythm placement IS the performance.*

### General Scale Pattern

Use a comfortable five-note scale:

**1–2–3–4–5–4–3–2–1**

Begin in an easy middle register. Move upward by a semitone only while the sound remains free and comfortable.

Each vowel should merge into the next. Avoid taking a new breath or making a new throat shape between every vowel.

### Practice Set 1: Open Back-Vowel Line

**Vowel pattern:** AH – OO – OW – OE – AW – OE – OO – AH
**Singable version:** Ah—oo—oh—ho—aw—ho—oo—ah

**Purpose:** prepares the back vowels of the title and refrain — *Kung, Fu, oh, ho, fought, down, boss*.

**Focus:** AH (*Kung*) stays open and easy; OO (*Fu*) rounds the lips without pulling the tongue back; the throat shape never changes. This AH→OO move opens the title phrase every time it appears — it must cost nothing.

### Practice Set 2: The "-ightning" Line

**Vowel pattern:** AY – IH | AY – IH | AY – IH | AY – IH
**Singable version:** Ay-ih—ay-ih—ay-ih—ay-ih

**Purpose:** the rhyme engine of the song — *fighting, lightning, frightening, timing* all land on AY → IH-ng.

**Focus:** The AY carries the note; the IH-ng is a light unstressed tail. Do not punch the second syllable — the word bounces, it doesn't stamp. Sustain: f-AAAY-t-ih-ng.

### Practice Set 3: Back-to-Front Bridge

**Vowel pattern:** AH – OO – AY – EE – AY – OO – AH
**Singable version:** Ah—oo—ay—ee—ay—oo—ah

**Purpose:** the song constantly alternates round back vowels (*Kung Fu*) with bright front vowels (*fighting, everybody*). This trains the switch without a gear change.

**Focus:** Equal fullness on every vowel; lips round for OO, tongue rises for EE, jaw stays released throughout.

### Practice Set 4: "Oh-Ho-Ho-Ho" Refrain

**Vowel pattern:** OW – OE – OE – OE, repeated
**Singable version:** Oh—ho—ho—ho

**Purpose:** the most repeated figure in the song — it opens the record, fills the ad-libs and runs the whole outro.

**Focus:** Take it **light** — a bouncy, heady, almost-laughing production, not a pushed chest sound. The *h* is a small puff of breath from the belly, not a squeeze at the throat; between the *h*s the OE vowels stay connected on one airstream, all on the same pitch centre. If the pitch dips on each *h*, the puffs are too big.

### Practice Set 5: The "Huh! / Ha!" Interjections

**Pattern:** h-AH! — short, percussive, on the beat
**Singable version:** huh! · ha! · huh! · ha!

**Purpose:** the interjections are percussion, and there are a lot of them — done wrong (glottal slam, or a big air dump) they tire the voice and drain the tank.

**Focus:** Each one is a quick abdominal pulse — the same "accent" action as the accent-breathing drills — with the throat staying open. Short, pitched low, released instantly. Practise eight in a row on a steady beat; the last one should feel exactly like the first, and your ribs should still be wide afterwards.

### Practice Set 6: Title Line Vowel Ribbon

**Lyric:** Everybody was Kung Fu fighting
**Vowel-only line:** EH – EE – AH – EE – AH – AH – OO – AY – IH
**Continuous exercise:** EH—EE—AH—EE—AH—AH—OO—AY—IH

**Focus:** One ribbon, nine vowels. The quick *everybody* vowels stay small and even; the line blooms only at OO–AY (*Fu figh-*). After the vowels connect, add consonants lightly:
🎤 EH-v-r-EE-b-AH-d-EE w-AH-z k-AH-ng f-OO f-AY-t-IH-ng

### Practice Set 7: "Fast as Lightning" Vowel Line

**Lyric:** Those cats were fast as lightning
**Vowel-only line:** OE – AE – UH – AE – AE – AY – IH
**Continuous exercise:** OE—AE—UH—AE—AE—AY—IH

**Focus:** Three AE vowels in a row (*cats, fast, as*) — keep them bright but shallow, no jaw press. UH (*were*) is a throwaway. The line drives forward to land on AY (*light-*).

Then: 🎤 th-OE-z k-AE-ts w-UH-r f-AE-st AE-z l-AY-t-n-IH-ng

### Practice Set 8: "Frightening" Line with Fillers

**Lyric:** In fact, it was a little bit frightening
**Vowel-only line:** IH – AE – IH – AH – OE – IH – OE – IH – AY – IH
**Continuous exercise:** IH—AE—IH—AH—OE—IH—OE—IH—AY—IH

**Focus:** Almost all small vowels until the payoff AY. The unstressed OEs (*a, -tle*) are near-invisible schwas. This is the wordiest chorus line — economy is everything: tiny mouth movements, forward placement, and let the rhythm do the work.

Then: 🎤 IH-n f-AE-kt IH-t w-AH-z OE l-IH-t-OE-l b-IH-t fr-AY-t-n-IH-ng

### Practice Set 9: "Expert Timing" Vowel Line

**Lyric:** But they fought with expert timing
**Vowel-only line:** AH – AY – AW – IH – EH – UH – AY – IH
**Continuous exercise:** AH—AY—AW—IH—EH—UH—AY—IH

**Focus:** AW (*fought*) is the one genuinely open back vowel in the chorus — enjoy it, but don't sit on it; the line must arrive on *timing* exactly on the beat. The word is a self-instruction.

Then: 🎤 b-AH-t th-AY f-AW-t w-IH-th EH-k-sp-UH-rt t-AY-m-IH-ng

### Practice Set 10: Internal-Rhyme Patter Line

**Lyric:** It's an ancient Chinese art, and everybody knew their part
**Vowel-only line:** IH – AE – AY – OE – AY – EE – AH – AE – EH – EE – AH – EE – OO – EH – AH
**Continuous exercise:** IH—AE—AY—OE—AY—EE—AH—AE—EH—EE—AH—EE—OO—EH—AH

**Purpose:** the fastest, longest lines in the song are these internal-rhyme couplets (also *"From a feint into a slip and a kicking from the hip"*). This is articulation-at-speed training.

**Focus:** Start at half tempo, vowels only, perfectly even. Speed up only while the vowels stay connected and unforced. The rhyme words (*art/part, slip/hip*) land on the beat; everything between them is light.

### Practice Set 11: Groove Placement

**Lyric:** They were chopping them up, they were chopping them down
**Vowel-only line:** AY – UH – AH – IH – EH – AH | AY – UH – AH – IH – EH – AW
**Continuous exercise:** with a metronome or the record — AY—UH—AH—IH—EH—AH / AY—UH—AH—IH—EH—AW

**Purpose:** trains placing the legato vowel ribbon *on the groove* — this song is funk: slightly behind the beat, bouncing, never rushed.

**Focus:** Clap or tap the backbeat (2 and 4) while singing the vowel line. The syllables *chop-* and *up/down* snap onto their beats; everything else swings between. If the vowels are connected but the placement drifts, the song isn't learned yet — in this number, timing is the instrument.

### Practice Set 12: "Keep On" Loop

**Lyric:** Keep on, keep on, keep on, keep on
**Vowel pattern:** EE – AH | EE – AH | EE – AH | EE – AH
**Singable version:** Kee-pon—kee-pon—kee-pon—kee-pon

**Purpose:** the outro chant — a stamina loop that must stay relaxed and rhythmic while the crowd energy peaks.

**Focus:** EE stays forward and easy, AH (*on*) stays open; a sneak breath is available after every second "keep on". Loop it for 30–60 seconds at performance tempo: the tenth repetition must sound identical to the first — that's the outro's real test.

## PART FOUR: SUGGESTED PRACTICE ROUTINE

### Stage 1: Gentle Release

Hum lightly on *mm* or *ng*. Open into mm—AH · mm—OO · ng—AY. No volume yet.

### Stage 2: Establish the Vowel Families

Sets 1–3 on the five-note scale, two or three times each — the AH→OO title move and the AY–IH rhyme engine.

### Stage 3: The Refrain and the Percussion

Sets 4 and 5 — "oh-ho-ho-ho" light and bouncy; "huh!/ha!" as abdominal pulses. Keep both small; they are seasoning, not the meal.

### Stage 4: Chorus Lines Without Consonants

Sets 6–9 in song rhythm, vowels only, below performance volume.

### Stage 5: Patter and Groove

Sets 10 and 11 with a metronome (or the record): half tempo → full tempo, backbeat on 2 and 4. Add Set 12 as the closer.

### Stage 6: Add Light Consonants, Then Character

Full lines with consonants touching lightly. Only then add the grin, the swagger and the interjections — character is the last layer, added over a groove that already runs itself.

## PART FIVE: FRONT VS BACK VOWEL SUMMARY

### Front vowels

**EE, IH, EH, AY and AE**

The strongest front-vowel patterns occur in:

- **AY:** fighting, lightning, China, timing, made and swaying
- **IH:** little, bit, kicking, skip, did and the final syllables of fighting and lightning
- **AE:** cats, fast, fact, Sammy, stand, hands and have
- **EE:** everybody, Chinese, Billy, he, me, feet and keep
- **EH:** expert, men, said, get, let's and them

These brighter vowels give the verses rhythmic clarity and help words such as *fighting, lightning, fast* and *expert* cut through the melody.

### Back vowels

**AH, UH, AO, OE, AW, OO and OW**

The strongest back-vowel patterns occur in:

- **AH:** Kung, was, funky, chopping, up, comes, sudden and the huh/ha interjections
- **OE:** the repeated *ho* syllables, plus unstressed words such as *a* and *the*
- **OW:** *oh* and the opening of the repeated vocal refrain
- **OO:** Fu, knew, new, you and into
- **AW:** fought, down, bow, now, Chinatown and boss
- **UH:** were, expert, took and sure

The song alternates bright AY/AE/EE vowels with rounder AH/OE/OO/OW vowels. The title phrase is especially balanced: *Kung* uses a broad back vowel, *Fu* uses a rounded back vowel, and *fighting* shifts into a bright front diphthong.

## PART SIX: WHAT THE SONG DEMANDS

*Measured from Aaron's two live takes (30 & 31 Jul 2026). No original-artist
reference is in the calibration pack yet, so the numbers describe the song as
Aaron sings it.*

### The measured range, as sung

| Measure | Take 1 (first live) | Take 2 (Brighton) |
|---|---|---|
| Working core | G3 – F♯4 | F♯3 – F♯4 |
| Most-lived-in note | **E4** (~32 s) | **E4** (~30 s) |
| Next most | F♯4, F4, D♯4 | F♯4, F4, D♯4 |
| Register balance | 84.5% light | 67.7% light |
| Delivery density | ~3.4 onsets/sec | ~4.05 onsets/sec |

The picture is consistent across both nights: the chant lives on **E4–F♯4 at
the top of the speech range**, sung mostly in a light production — which is
the right call for this song. The extremes the tracker logged are brief
flicks and ad-lib noise, not sustained demands. Roughly four vocal onsets
per second confirms this is an articulation song, not a sustain song.

### The one measured weakness: held notes that wander

Both takes tell the same story, and it is *not* range or breath — it is
**held-note drift**. The chant's sustained syllables (the AY of *fight-ing /
light-ning / fright-ening / tim-ing*, sitting on E4/F4/F♯4) land on pitch
and then wander instead of sitting still: median intra-note drift of 41.6
cents on take 1, improving to 31.2 cents at Brighton. The trouble-spot lists
cluster exactly there — repeated F4 holds through the choruses drifting
150–200 cents. The note histogram shows the fingerprint: E4, F4 and F♯4
*all* heavily used — three adjacent semitones where the melody really wants
two pitch centres, cleanly held.

The engine's prescribed fix (take 1's primary focus) is **Messa di Voce on
single pitches** — *still first, shape second*: settle each sustained chant
note dead-centre for its first two seconds before adding any swell, bounce
or character. Practice Set 2 (the "-ightning" line) is where to apply it.

Also measured: ~40% of onsets are scooped (median ~1 semitone). On a song
made of short notes, a scoop eats the whole note — the blueprint rule (*land
the note first*) applies at patter speed.

### The shape of the job

This is a different animal from the two Farnham guides. There is almost no
long-sustain singing in it: the melody is narrow, chant-like and sits at the
top of the speech range, moving in short rhythmic cells. Nothing demands a
held climax note. The demands are, in order:

1. **Groove** — funk placement, relaxed and slightly behind the beat, for
   three-plus minutes without rushing. In this song timing *is* the
   instrument; the engine's timing/groove measures will grade exactly this
   when a take lands.
2. **Articulation at speed** — the internal-rhyme patter couplets
   (*art/part*, *slip/hip*, *stand/hand*, *skip/trip*) are the hardest lines.
3. **Character stamina** — the huh!s, ha!s and oh-ho-ho-hos run the whole
   song and must sound as fresh in the outro as the intro.
4. **A light upper flick** — the "oh-ho-ho-ho" refrain sits above the spoken
   register and wants a light, heady, bouncing production, not chest push.

### Fit

Squarely in the wheelhouse. The current blueprint calls the voice "bright,
forward and twang-capable; naturally suited to rock, pub rock, blues, funk
and speech-connected material" — this is a speech-connected funk number, the
same skill family as the measured repertoire strengths *Play That Funky
Music* and *Funky Cold Medina*. Original key should be fine; the melody's
narrow range makes transposition a non-issue.

The one blueprint caution that transfers: **don't add grit and swagger until
the groove and pitch centres are secure.** Character first is the trap;
character *last* is the method (Part Four, Stage 6).

### Style targets

- Delivery is rhythmic speech-song: forward placement, conversational
  weight, clean onsets. No scooping — the notes are short, so a scooped
  onset eats the whole note.
- The refrain "oh-ho-ho-ho" is the sonic signature: light, held-back, almost
  amused. Restraint there is what makes the groove feel deep.
- Dynamics live in the band, not the voice: the vocal stays in a narrow,
  confident band and lets the arrangement move.

## PART SEVEN: BREATH CONTROL MAP

*Measured: take 1 — 39 phrases, median 3.7 s, 48.7% of endings falling;
take 2 (Brighton) — 23 phrases, median 5.3 s, 43.5% falling. Phrases are
short, and in a funk chant many falling endings are stylistic releases
rather than leaks — the caveat from the repo's sag-diagnosis work applies
in full here. The real breath signal in this song is not the endings; it is
the held-note drift covered in Part Six (drift is the classic sound of
breath pressure decaying under a note) plus the interjection economy below.*

This is not a long-phrase song; it is a **death-by-a-thousand-cuts** song.
No single line challenges the tank, but the interjections, refrains and
patter run continuously with few real rests — small leaks compound.

### The two leak points

1. **The interjections.** Every huh!/ha! is an air expense. As abdominal
   pulses (Set 5) they cost almost nothing; as throaty barks they drain the
   tank and tire the folds. There are dozens of them — the technique
   difference compounds over the song.
2. **The "oh-ho-ho-ho" refrains.** Each *h* is a small breath spend — four
   per refrain, refrains stacked back-to-back in the intro, ad-libs and
   outro. Keep the puffs tiny and the vowels connected; top up between
   refrain groups, not mid-figure.

### Where the breaths go

- **Verses/choruses** — a relaxed catch breath at every line break. Lines
  are 3–4 s; this is comfortable. The discipline is *taking* them —
  groove excitement makes singers skip breaths they had time for.
- **Patter couplets** — the long ones split at the comma: "It's an ancient
  Chinese art, // and everybody knew their part"; "We took a bow and made a
  stand, // started swaying with the hand". Mark the mid-line breath and
  keep it every time.
- **Ad-lib section** — breathe **between** refrain groups: "(oh-ho-ho-ho)
  ha!" → breath → next group. Never chain two refrains plus interjections on
  one tank.
- **Outro** — the loop ("keep on…" + refrains) is the stamina test. Set 12's
  rule applies: sneak a breath every second "keep on", and keep the
  interjections as pulses. The outro should end with air to spare — it's the
  last thing the room hears.

### Training

- Set 5 (interjection pulses) and Set 4 (refrain puffs) *are* the breath
  training for this song — plus the accent-breathing drill in
  `aaroncodex/vocal-knowledge-base/01-vocal-science-technique/accent-splat-breathing-for-singing.md`,
  which is exactly the huh!-pulse action.
- The straw warm-up from the Pressure Down routine transfers as a general
  reset, but the key skill here is **many small even spends**, not one long
  supported line.

## PART EIGHT: SPECIFIC PREPARATION PLAN

Lighter than the Farnham plans — one week of focused sessions gets this
performance-ready, because nothing here fights the voice. The work is groove
and economy.

### Days 1–2 — Vowels and the two signatures

- Stages 1–3: vowel families, then Sets 4 and 5 until the refrain is light
  and the interjections are pulses. These two sounds are the song's identity;
  get them cheap first.

### Days 3–4 — Lines on the grid

- Sets 6–9 vowels-only in rhythm, then with consonants.
- Sets 10–11 with a metronome: half tempo → full tempo, backbeat claps.
  The patter couplets are the only genuinely hard lines — give them the most
  repetitions.

### Days 5–6 — Full runs and character

- Two full runs per session with the record, then one without it
  (backing-track or a cappella over a metronome): the groove must live in
  you, not be borrowed from Carl Douglas.
- Add character last: the grin, the swagger, the ha!s at performance energy.
- **Messa di voce transfer** (the engine's prescribed drill for this song):
  three comfortable chant notes (E4 area), four seconds each, dead still for
  the first two seconds, one small swell, return — then one chorus at 70%
  keeping every held AY centred before any bounce is added. The measured
  target: held-note drift trending down take-over-take (41.6 → 31.2 cents so
  far; the pro-reference median is ~24).

### Day 7 — Performance simulation

- One full run at performance energy, standing, moving. The outro loop at
  full crowd-energy, ending with air in the tank.

### Performance-night notes (live/karaoke)

- This is a **room** song: the crowd will do the "oh-ho-ho-ho"s and the
  huh!s with you. Let them — conduct the interjections rather than carrying
  them all, and bank the air.
- Keep the verses conversational and behind the beat; rushing is the only
  way to ruin this song. If the room's energy pulls the tempo, lock onto the
  drummer/backing track backbeat, not the crowd.
- The moves are part of the song — but set the groove with the voice first,
  then add the chops. A dropped line for a karate pose is a fair trade
  exactly once.

## FINAL SINGING REMINDER

🎤 Let the vowel carry the note.
🎤 Let the consonant briefly shape the word.
🎤 The groove is the instrument — place every vowel on it.
🎤 Interjections are belly pulses, never throat slams.
🎤 Light on the "ho"s: restraint is what makes funk deep.
🎤 Character goes on last, over a groove that already runs itself.
