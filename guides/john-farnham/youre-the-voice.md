## How to Use This Workbook

The vowel transcription is based on a neutral sung-English pronunciation. Individual vowels may change slightly according to accent, pitch and interpretation.

Consonants appear in lowercase. The principal sung vowels appear as capitalised codes.

When practising:

- Keep the jaw released rather than pushed downward.
- Allow the tongue and lips to form each vowel without squeezing the throat.
- Imagine one continuous stream of breath connecting the vowels.
- Do not try to hold the throat artificially wide.
- Practise softly before increasing volume or pitch.
- Stop if you experience pain, scraping, persistent hoarseness or throat pressure.

## PART ONE: VOWEL KEY

### Front Vowels

- **EE** — as in meet, we, believing
- **IH** — as in sit, live, it
- **EH** — as in ends, get, better
- **AY** — as in make, write, time
- **AE** — as in have, chance, can

### Back Vowels

- **AH** — as in the unstressed vowels of the, gonna, over
- **UH** — as in look
- **AO** — as in all, daughter, voice
- **OE** — as in over, know, older
- **AW** — as in how, down, power
- **OO** — as in food
- **OW** — a central or curled vowel, used here in turn

### Vowel Glides

Some words contain two connected vowel sounds:

- **AO–IH** — voice, noise
- **EE–AH** — clear, fear, we're
- **AW–AH** — power

The first vowel normally receives most of the sustained note. The second vowel is introduced gently near the end.

## PART THREE: VOWEL PRACTICE SETS

### General Scale Pattern

Use a comfortable five-note scale:

**1–2–3–4–5–4–3–2–1**

Begin in an easy middle register. Move upward by a semitone only while the sound remains free and comfortable.

Each vowel should merge into the next. Avoid taking a new breath or making a new throat shape between every vowel.

### Practice Set 1: Open Back-Vowel Line

**Vowel pattern:** AH – AO – OE – AW – OE – AO – AH
**Singable version:** Ah—aw—oh—ow—oh—aw—ah

**Purpose:** prepares the back vowels used in words such as *all, daughter, voice, know, how, down, older*.

**Focus:** Begin with an easy AH and retain its internal space as the lips gradually round. The lips may move, but the throat should not tighten when moving into AO, OE or AW.

### Practice Set 2: Back-to-Front Vowel Bridge

**Vowel pattern:** AH – AO – EH – EE – EH – AO – AH
**Singable version:** Ah—aw—eh—ee—eh—aw—ah

**Purpose:** helps the voice move from the open back vowels of the chorus into brighter front vowels without losing resonance.

**Focus:** Do not grin widely on EE. Allow the tongue to rise and move forward while the jaw remains released. Try to keep the tone equally full on every vowel.

### Practice Set 3: Complete Vowel Arc

**Vowel pattern:** AH – AO – OE – EE – AY – AE – AH, then reversed: AH – AE – AY – EE – OE – AO – AH
**Singable version:** Ah—aw—oh—ee—ay—ae—ah · Ah—ae—ay—ee—oh—aw—ah

**Purpose:** travels across the major front and back vowel families used throughout the song.

**Focus:** Imagine that all the vowels sit on the same breath and in the same resonant space. The tongue and lips change the vowel; the throat does not need to repeatedly open and close.

### Practice Set 4: "Voice" and "Noise" Glide

**Vowel pattern:** AO–IH | AO–IH | AO–IH | AO–IH
**Singable version:** Aw-ih—aw-ih—aw-ih—aw-ih
**Song words:** voice 🎤 v-AO-IH-s · noise 🎤 n-AO-IH-z

**Purpose:** develops the diphthong in the two important chorus words.

**Focus:** Sustain most of the note on AO. Move briefly toward IH only near the end of the note. Do not sing both parts of the diphthong with equal weight.

Practise: v-AOOOO-IH-s · n-AOOOO-IH-z

### Practice Set 5: "Clear" and "Fear" Glide

**Vowel pattern:** EE–AH | EE–AH | EE–AH | EE–AH
**Singable version:** Ee-ah—ee-ah—ee-ah—ee-ah
**Song words:** clear 🎤 kl-EE-AH · fear 🎤 f-EE-AH

**Purpose:** prepares the sustained ending words in the chorus.

**Focus:** Sustain the EE and allow a small, relaxed movement toward AH at the end. Avoid pulling the mouth sideways or allowing the final AH to become heavy.

Practise: kl-EEEE-AH · f-EEEE-AH

### Practice Set 6: Mixed Chorus Glides

**Vowel pattern:** AO–IH | AO–IH | EE–AH | EE–AH
**Singable version:** Aw-ih—aw-ih—ee-ah—ee-ah
**Song association:** voice—noise—clear—fear

**Purpose:** links the four most important sustained chorus words.

**Focus:** Maintain a consistent airflow and volume across all four vowel glides. Let the ending part of each glide remain light.

### Practice Set 7: "You're the Voice" Vowel Line

**Lyric:** You're the voice, try and understand it
**Vowel-only line:** AO – AH – AO–IH – AY – AE – AH – AH – AE – IH
**Continuous exercise:** AO—AH—AO-IH—AY—AE—AH—AH—AE—IH

**Focus:** Sing the entire pattern as one ribbon of sound. Pay particular attention to the change from the rounded AO of *voice* into the brighter AY of *try*.

After the vowels feel connected, add the consonants lightly:
🎤 y-AO th-AH v-AO-IH-s tr-AY AE-nd AH-n-d-AH-st-AE-nd IH-t

### Practice Set 8: "Make a Noise" Vowel Line

**Lyric:** Make a noise and make it clear
**Vowel-only line:** AY – AH – AO–IH – AE – AY – IH – EE–AH
**Continuous exercise:** AY—AH—AO-IH—AE—AY—IH—EE-AH

**Focus:** Keep *make*, *noise* and *clear* connected rather than treating them as separate vocal shapes. Sustain AO in *noise* and EE in *clear*. Introduce the ending vowels only near the release.

After the vowels feel even, add the consonants:
🎤 m-AY-k AH n-AO-IH-z AE-nd m-AY-k IH-t kl-EE-AH

### Practice Set 9: "We're Not Gonna" Vowel Line

**Lyric:** We're not gonna sit in silence
**Vowel-only line:** EE–AH – AO – AH – AH – IH – IH – AY – AH
**Continuous exercise:** EE-AH—AO—AH—AH—IH—IH—AY—AH

**Focus:** Keep the repeated AH vowels free and speech-like. Do not press the jaw or throat on *not* or *gonna*. Save the brighter resonance for IH in *sit* and AY in *silence*.

### Practice Set 10: "Live With Fear" Vowel Line

**Lyric:** We're not gonna live with fear
**Vowel-only line:** EE–AH – AO – AH – AH – IH – IH – EE–AH
**Continuous exercise:** EE-AH—AO—AH—AH—IH—IH—EE-AH

**Focus:** Allow the two IH vowels in *live with* to remain clear without becoming tight. Approach the final EE–AH in *fear* with steady breath rather than extra throat pressure.

### Practice Set 11: "Oh-Wo-Wo-Wo" Resonance Line

**Vowel pattern:** OE – OE – OE – OE | AH – OE – OE – OE
**Singable version:** Oh—oh—oh—oh | wah—oh—oh—oh

**Purpose:** prepares the repeated chorus vocalisation and encourages an open, ringing sound.

**Focus:** Keep the lips rounded but flexible. Avoid pushing the tongue backward on OE. Let the vowel resonate forward while the throat remains released. Begin softly and increase intensity only if the sound stays easy.

### Practice Set 12: Verse Connection Line

**Lyric:** This time we know we all can stand together
**Vowel-only line:** IH – AY – EE – OE – EE – AO – AE – AE – AH – EH – AH
**Continuous exercise:** IH—AY—EE—OE—EE—AO—AE—AE—AH—EH—AH

**Purpose:** practises the faster sequence of changing vowels in the second verse.

**Focus:** Do not over-form every vowel. Keep the movement economical, connected and rhythmically even. Add the consonants only after the vowel sequence can be sung without interruption.

## PART FOUR: SUGGESTED PRACTICE ROUTINE

### Stage 1: Gentle Release

Hum lightly on *mm* or *ng* for several comfortable notes. Allow the vibration to feel easy around the lips, nose and front of the face.

Open from the hum into: mm—AH · mm—OE · ng—EE

Do not increase volume yet.

### Stage 2: Establish the Open Vowels

Sing Practice Sets 1 and 2 on a comfortable five-note scale. Repeat each set two or three times. The goal is not maximum volume. The goal is an even, connected sound.

### Stage 3: Practise the Diphthongs

Sing Practice Sets 4, 5 and 6. Keep the first vowel long and the second vowel short: AO——IH · EE——AH

### Stage 4: Practise the Chorus Without Consonants

Sing Practice Sets 7, 8, 9 and 10 using vowels only. Use the actual rhythm of the song, but begin below full performance volume.

### Stage 5: Add Light Consonants

Return to the complete chorus. Allow consonants to touch the flowing vowel line rather than interrupting it.

Think: **continuous vowels with brief consonant contacts** rather than: separate words pushed from the throat.

### Stage 6: Add Performance Energy

Increase intensity gradually. A powerful sound should feel supported by steady breath and clear resonance — not by squeezing the neck, tongue or jaw.

## PART SIX: WHAT THE SONG DEMANDS — RANGE AND REGISTERS

*Measured from the engine's reference analysis of Farnham's separated vocal
(time-weighted note histogram over the full 5:11 stem).*

### The measured range

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Extremes touched | **D♯3 – A5** |
| Comfortable core of the performance | **C4 – F♯5** |
| Most-lived-in note | **F4** (36 s of the song), then G4 (22 s), A4 (17 s), B♭4 (13 s) |
| Chorus tessitura | **F4 – C5** |
| Climactic ad-lib territory | **F5 – G5** (G5 alone carries ~13 s; peaks touch A5) |
| Estimated passaggio in the original | **~B4** (register flips cluster there) |

Read that table honestly: the danger in this song is **not** one high note. It
is that the chorus *lives* between F4 and C5 for line after line, and then the
final section climbs a full fourth higher again for the ad-libs. Farnham sings
roughly 86% of the song in full voice and only 14% in a lighter/head setup —
that residency in full voice at A4–C5 is the athletic feat.

### What that means for a G3–A♯4-core voice

Aaron's current profile (see
`aaroncodex/vocal-knowledge-base/06-voxai-system/aaron-vocal-blueprint-v2.md`):
comfortable working core ~G3–A♯4, transition zone ~A4–C5. The chorus tessitura
of this song sits **exactly on top of that transition zone** — the song asks you
to *live* where the voice most wants to grab weight. Three workable routes:

1. **Drop the key 2–3 semitones (karaoke key change).** −2 puts the chorus at
   E♭4–B♭4 (top of core); −3 puts it at D4–A4, comfortably inside the core and
   leaves headroom for the ad-libs an octave down or lightened. This is the
   recommended starting point for performance while the song is new.
2. **Original key, lighter chorus setup.** Take the chorus in a bright,
   speech-connected mix rather than hauling chest weight up — the blueprint's
   "lighter upper setup is currently more reliable" finding applies directly.
   The vowel workbook helps here: the dominant back vowels (AO/OE) keep space
   without spreading.
3. **Hybrid:** original key for verses/pre-chorus (they sit C4–F4, fine),
   planned lighter mix for choruses, and a **pre-decided** substitution for the
   F5–G5 ad-libs (octave down, or hand them to the crowd — this song invites
   it). Never improvise that decision mid-song.

### Style targets measured from the original

- **Vibrato:** ~5.7 Hz rate, ~50 cents extent, arriving essentially immediately
  on sustained notes. That is a warm, moderate vibrato — not a slow wobble, not
  a fast flutter. Target it on *clear*, *fear*, *voice*, *noise* and the
  "oh-wo" sustains.
- **Onsets:** Farnham scoops into roughly a quarter of his onsets (median scoop
  ~1 semitone) and rides over the top of others — it is deliberate style. Per
  the blueprint rule, **land the note first**; add the scoop as a choice only
  after the centre is secure.
- **Dynamics:** the original uses ~24 dB of effective dynamic range. Verses are
  conversational; the full send is saved for the choruses and above all the
  post-bagpipe final section. Copy the *shape*, not just the loud end.

## PART SEVEN: BREATH CONTROL MAP

*Phrase data measured from the original vocal: 28 phrases, median phrase
length **5.6 seconds**, longest chained passage **22.7 seconds** (lines joined
with catch-breath gaps too short for the analyser to split). Notably, even
Farnham's own take shows sagging endings on 8 of 28 phrases — including the
long chained passages late in the song (measured sags of 40–250 cents at
~3:27 and ~4:20, and a big stylistic fall-off at ~3:53). The song genuinely
drains the tank at the end; it must be budgeted for, not powered through.*

This song is close to a worst case for phrase-ending sag (the current primary
coaching target): notes that land and then slide flat as the phrase runs out of
air. The counter is planned breath placement, not bigger gulps.

### Where the breaths go

- **Verses** — easy. Breathe at the punctuation; phrases are 3–5 s and sit low.
  Use them to stay relaxed, not to bank tension.
- **Pre-chorus** — the trap. "How long can we look at each other / down the
  barrel of a gun" wants to be one dramatic arc. Don't. Take a real rib-wide
  breath **after "other"**, before "down the barrel". The pause reads as drama;
  running dry on "gun" reads as weakness.
- **Chorus** — mark these and keep them every single time:
  - breath after "understand it"
  - breath after "clear" (let the EE–AH release finish first — don't clip it)
  - **sneak breaths between each "oh-wo-wo-wo" group.** The `w` consonant is a
    natural reset point; a small top-up there is inaudible. Never chain both
    "oh-wo" lines on one tank.
  - breath after "silence" — do not carry into "we're not gonna live with fear"
  - "fear" is a long EE–AH sustain at the top of the phrase: it gets the most
    air of any word in the chorus. Arrive there with reserves, not fumes.
- **Post-bagpipe section** — the endurance test: pre-chorus + final choruses
  back-to-back with the biggest ad-libs, at the point of maximum fatigue. Sing
  the section *before* it (second chorus) at 80%, arrive with a full tank, and
  keep the marked breaths even when excitement says skip them.

### Breath training for this song specifically

1. **Ribs-wide anchor:** the blueprint cue — *land the note, keep the ribs
   wide, then let the colour arrive.* Practise every chorus line with a hand on
   the lower ribs; the ribs should still be wide at the *last word*, not
   collapsed by mid-phrase.
2. **Phrase-length ladder:** sing Practice Sets 7–10 (vowels only) at low
   volume, one line per breath. Then two lines per breath. The point is not to
   perform without breathing — it is to build enough margin that the *marked*
   breaths leave you finishing phrases with air to spare.
3. **Sag audit:** record one chorus, listen only to the final second of each
   phrase. Any ending that droops flat gets its preceding breath enlarged or
   moved earlier. (For a full measured audit, run a take through the
   aaroncodex engine — its breath section measures exactly this,
   phrase-by-phrase, in cents.)

## PART EIGHT: SPECIFIC PREPARATION PLAN

A suggested two-week shape, assuming the vowel workbook (Parts One–Four) is
already in daily use. Each session ~25–35 minutes.

### Days 1–3 — Decide the key, own the verses

- Warm up with Part Four Stages 1–2.
- Test the chorus at original key, −2 and −3 with Practice Set 7 on the actual
  melody, quietly. Pick the key where the *last* line of a double chorus is
  still comfortable — not the first.
- Learn verses and pre-chorus at performance volume; they should feel easy and
  conversational. Lock the pre-chorus breath after "other".

### Days 4–7 — Chorus residency

- Practice Sets 4–6 (diphthongs), then Sets 7–10 in song rhythm, vowels only,
  at the chosen key.
- Add consonants (Stage 5) at 70% volume. Marked breaths every repetition.
- One "sag audit" recording at the end of each session (Part Seven §3).
- Register work: 10 minutes of mixed-voice sirens through A4–C5 (see
  `mixed-voice-development-exercises.md`) — the goal is that F4–C5 stops
  feeling like a ceiling and starts feeling like a floor you stand on lightly.

### Days 8–11 — The "oh-wo" engine and the ending

- Practice Set 11 daily; build from soft to ringing without the throat joining
  in. This figure repeats more than anything else in the song — it must cost
  almost nothing.
- Run second-half-only passes: bagpipe entry → final chorus, starting fresh.
  Then run them *after* a full first half, and compare. The gap between those
  two is the stamina debt to train down.
- Decide and rehearse the ad-lib plan (octave-down / crowd handoff / lightened
  top). Rehearse the *chosen* plan only.

### Days 12–14 — Full runs and performance energy

- Two full runs per session, first at 80%, second at performance energy
  (Stage 6). Keep every marked breath.
- Vibrato check on *clear/fear/voice/noise*: aim for the measured original
  feel — settles fast, ~5–6 gentle cycles per second, no wobble.
- Final run: record it. If phrase endings hold pitch and the last chorus still
  has "oh-wo"s in the tank, the song is performance-ready.

### Performance-night notes (live/karaoke)

- This is a marathon with a sprint finish: budget energy so the post-bagpipe
  section is the peak, not the survival lap.
- The crowd **will** sing the "oh-wo"s — let them carry alternate ones in the
  final choruses and bank the air.
- Mic off the chin for the F4–C5 chorus lines (they carry themselves); closer
  for the low conversational verses.
- Bail-out plan, pre-decided: if the voice tires early, the final-chorus
  ad-libs drop the octave. Nobody notices a plan; everybody notices a crack.

## FINAL SINGING REMINDER

🎤 Let the vowel carry the note.
🎤 Let the consonant briefly shape the word.
🎤 Keep the breath moving through every vowel change.
🎤 Allow the lips and tongue to move while the jaw and throat remain released.
🎤 Power should come from coordinated breath and resonance, not throat pressure.
🎤 Land the note first. Keep the ribs wide. Then let the colour arrive.
