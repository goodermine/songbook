# How to Sing: Pressure Down — John Farnham

> **Catalogue entry:** Pressure Down, John Farnham
> **From:** *Whispering Jack* (1986)
>
> This guide has two halves. **Parts One–Five** are the vowel workbook: the
> complete song vowel breakdown and twelve legato practice sets built from it.
> **Parts Six–Eight** are the song-demands layer: measured range, breath
> control map and specific preparation. Unlike *You're the Voice* (measured
> from the original Farnham vocal), the measurements here come from the
> vox-analysis engine's analyses of **Aaron's own nine takes of this song** —
> including the Captain Cook Tavern live take, whose breath diagnosis produced
> the drill sheet `aaroncodex/docs/practice/pressure-down-breath-support.pdf`.
> Raw measures only; no scores live in this document.

---

## How to Use This Workbook

The vowel transcription is based on a neutral sung-English pronunciation. Individual vowels may change slightly according to accent, pitch and interpretation.

Consonants appear in lowercase. The principal sung vowels appear as capitalised codes.

When practising:

- Keep the jaw released rather than pushed downward.
- Allow the tongue and lips to form each vowel without squeezing the throat.
- Imagine one continuous stream of breath connecting the vowels.
- Do not try to hold the throat artificially wide.
- Practise softly before increasing volume or pitch.
- Stop if you experience pain, scraping, persistent hoarseness or throat pressure.

## PART ONE: VOWEL KEY

### Front Vowels

- **EE** — as in wheels, weary, feel, city, lead, winner
- **IH** — as in in, this, rising, winner, prisoner
- **EH** — as in set, pressure, them, let, tells
- **AY** — as in take, sail, rising, like, name, pain, eyes
- **AE** — as in and, can, trapped

### Back and Central Vowels

- **AH** — as in the unstressed vowels of the, pressure, someone, comes
- **UH** — as in full
- **AO** — as in storm, called, story, glory
- **OE** — as in motion, grown, hold, lonely
- **AW** — as in down, around, town
- **OO** — as in you, loser, to
- **OW** — the short open o, as in watch, across, on, gone
- **ER** — the curled central vowel, as in turn, turning, were

*Note for singers of the* You're the Voice *workbook: this song adds **ER**
(turn), and **OW** here means the short open o of watch/gone rather than a
curled vowel.*

### Vowel Glides

- **AY** is itself a glide (ah→ee). Sustain the open first part; close to the
  ee only at the release: t-AAAA(y)-k.
- **AW** in *down/around/town* opens wide and closes toward oo. Sustain the
  open part; the closing movement belongs to the last instant before the *n*.
- **AW–AH** — our (*our separate lives*): most of the note on AW, a gentle AH
  turn at the end.

## PART THREE: VOWEL PRACTICE SETS

*Same method as the* You're the Voice *workbook: every set is a legato line —
one ribbon of breath, vowels merging into each other, consonants added only
after the vowel line is seamless.*

### General Scale Pattern

Use a comfortable five-note scale:

**1–2–3–4–5–4–3–2–1**

Begin in an easy middle register. Move upward by a semitone only while the sound remains free and comfortable.

Each vowel should merge into the next. Avoid taking a new breath or making a new throat shape between every vowel.

### Practice Set 1: Open Back-Vowel Line

**Vowel pattern:** AH – OW – AO – OE – AW – OE – AO – AH
**Singable version:** Ah—wo—aw—oh—ow—oh—aw—ah

**Purpose:** prepares the back vowels used in words such as *watch, on, gone, storm, called, story, motion, hold, down, around, town*.

**Focus:** Begin with an easy AH and keep its internal space as the lips shape each new vowel. OW (*watch*) is short and open — do not round it into AO. The throat stays released throughout; only the lips and tongue move.

### Practice Set 2: The AY Spine

**Vowel pattern:** AY – AY – AY – AY on the scale, then AH – AY – AH – AY
**Singable version:** Ay—ay—ay—ay · Ah—ay—ah—ay

**Purpose:** AY is the song's signature bright vowel — *take, sail, rising, like, name, pain, eyes, hides*. The chorus starts on it every single line.

**Focus:** Sing AY as a long open "ah-flavoured" vowel that closes to ee only at the very end of the note. If every AY closes early, the chorus turns thin and tight. Keep the jaw released; the brightness comes from the tongue, not a spread mouth.

### Practice Set 3: Back-to-Front Bridge

**Vowel pattern:** AW – AH – EH – EE – EH – AH – AW
**Singable version:** Ow—ah—eh—ee—eh—ah—ow

**Purpose:** moves between the chorus's wide back vowels (*down*) and its bright front vowels (*pressure, feel*) without changing the throat shape.

**Focus:** The step from AH to EH to EE should feel like the tongue rising through one continuous space. Keep the tone equally full on every vowel — do not let EE go small.

### Practice Set 4: The ER Curl

**Vowel pattern:** AH – ER – AH – ER – AH
**Singable version:** Ah—er—ah—er—ah

**Purpose:** trains the central ER of *turn, turning, turned, were* — a vowel the *You're the Voice* workbook never needed.

**Focus:** ER is the vowel most likely to grip: singers curl the tongue back hard and squeeze. Let ER be a coloured AH — tongue slightly bunched, lips relaxed, no pressing. If the throat tightens, sing AH and add only the minimum curl that makes the word readable.

### Practice Set 5: "Take" Release

**Vowel pattern:** AY——k | AY——k | AY——k
**Singable version:** Taaaay-k · saaaay-l · naaaay-m · paaaay-n

**Purpose:** the sustained AY words — *take, sail, name, pain*.

**Focus:** Sustain the open part of AY on full breath, close briefly to the ee, then let the final consonant be a light touch at the release — never a push. The consonant ends the note; it does not end the breath.

### Practice Set 6: "Down" Release — the hook

**Vowel pattern:** AW——n | AW——n | AW——n
**Singable version:** Dooooww-n · toooww-n · arooooww-n(d)

**Purpose:** *down* is the most important sustained word in the song and the exact place the measured takes lose pitch (see Part Seven).

**Focus:** Three jobs, in order:
1. **Land** the note dead-centre — no sliding up into it.
2. **Hold** the open AW with the ribs wide; the pitch stays level for the
   whole sustain. A hand on the lower ribs — they must still be wide when the
   *n* arrives.
3. **Release** — close gently to *n*. If you *choose* a falling release for
   style, make the fall after the note has been held level, and make it the
   same size every time. A chosen fall is style; a drifting slide is a leak.

### Practice Set 7: "Take the Pressure Down" Vowel Line

**Lyric:** Take the pressure down
**Vowel-only line:** AY – AH – EH – AH – AW
**Continuous exercise:** AY—AH—EH—AH—AW

**Focus:** One ribbon of sound, five vowels, one breath. The line travels bright-to-dark: AY at the front, AW at the end. Do not let the tone change gear between EH and AW — same space, rounder lips.

After the vowels feel connected, add the consonants lightly:
🎤 t-AY-k th-AH pr-EH-sh-AH d-AW-n

### Practice Set 8: "Rising Like a Storm" Vowel Line

**Lyric:** 'Cause I can feel it, it's rising like a storm
**Vowel-only line:** AH – AY – AE – EE – IH – IH – AY – IH – AY – AH – AO
**Continuous exercise:** AH—AY—AE—EE—IH—IH—AY—IH—AY—AH—AO

**Focus:** The longest chorus line. Keep the quick middle vowels (IH-IH, IH) light and speech-like; save the fullness for EE in *feel* and AO in *storm*. Sustain AO, then let *rm* close the note.

After the vowels feel even, add the consonants:
🎤 k-AH-z AY k-AE-n f-EE-l IH-t IH-ts r-AY-z-IH-ng l-AY-k AH st-AO-rm

### Practice Set 9: "Take Hold of the Wheels" Vowel Line

**Lyric:** Take hold of the wheels and turn them around
**Vowel-only line:** AY – OE – AH – AH – EE – AE – ER – EH – AH – AW
**Continuous exercise:** AY—OE—AH—AH—EE—AE—ER—EH—AH—AW

**Focus:** This line contains the widest vowel journey in the song — bright AY, round OE, forward EE, central ER, wide AW. Practise it slowly as pure legato before attempting song tempo. The ER in *turn* must not interrupt the ribbon (Set 4).

Then add consonants:
🎤 t-AY-k h-OE-ld AH-v th-AH w-EE-lz AE-nd t-ER-n th-EH-m AH-r-AW-nd

### Practice Set 10: Bridge Lines

**Lyric:** Someone turned the pressure on / I called your name and you were gone
**Vowel-only lines:**
AH – AH – ER – AH – EH – AH – OW
AY – AO – AO – AY – AE – OO – ER – OW

**Continuous exercise:** AH—AH—ER—AH—EH—AH—OW · AY—AO—AO—AY—AE—OO—ER—OW

**Focus:** The bridge is the emotional turn of the song and sits in easy range — the risk is pushing for drama with the throat. Keep the repeated AH free, land OW (*on, gone*) open and short, and let intensity come from words, not squeeze.

### Practice Set 11: Fast Connection Line

**Lyric:** And I was trapped like a prisoner in this lonely town
**Vowel-only line:** AE – AY – AH – AE – AY – AH – IH – AH – AH – IH – IH – OE – EE – AW
**Continuous exercise:** AE—AY—AH—AE—AY—AH—IH—AH—AH—IH—IH—OE—EE—AW

**Purpose:** the fastest, wordiest line in the song — fourteen vowels ending on a sustained AW (*town*).

**Focus:** Economical vowel shapes at speed; do not over-form. The whole run is the approach path to *town* — arrive there with air to spare so the final AW can be held level (Set 6 rules apply).

### Practice Set 12: Verse Connection Line

**Lyric:** I want to sail across the ocean
**Vowel-only line:** AY – OW – OO – AY – AH – OW – EE – OE – AH
**Continuous exercise:** AY—OW—OO—AY—AH—OW—EE—OE—AH

**Purpose:** practises the verse's alternation of bright AY and open back vowels within one relaxed, conversational phrase.

**Focus:** Verses in this song are storytelling — sing this set at speaking effort. The contrast between easy verses and a committed chorus *is* the arrangement; do not spend chorus energy here.

## PART FOUR: SUGGESTED PRACTICE ROUTINE

### Stage 1: Gentle Release

Hum lightly on *mm* or *ng* for several comfortable notes. Open from the hum into: mm—AH · mm—AW · ng—AY

Do not increase volume yet.

### Stage 2: Straw Reset (song-specific)

Two or three rounds of straw phonation in water (the warm-up from the
Pressure Down drill sheet): blow gentle bubbles on a comfortable pitch, ribs
wide, and keep the bubbles **even**. The moment they go uneven, the rep is
over. This is the one warm-up that directly rehearses the airflow this song's
endings need.

### Stage 3: Establish the Vowel Families

Practice Sets 1–4 on a comfortable five-note scale, two or three times each. Even and connected beats loud.

### Stage 4: The Sustained Releases

Practice Sets 5 and 6 — *take* and *down*. Land, hold level, release. This is the heart of the song; give it more repetitions than anything else.

### Stage 5: Chorus Without Consonants

Practice Sets 7, 8 and 9 in song rhythm, vowels only, below performance volume.

### Stage 6: Add Light Consonants

Full chorus lines. Continuous vowels with brief consonant contacts — never separate words pushed from the throat.

### Stage 7: Add Performance Energy

Increase intensity gradually. Power comes from steady breath and resonance, not from squeezing on the hook.

## PART FIVE: FRONT VS BACK VOWEL SUMMARY

### Front Vowels

**EE, IH, EH, AY, AE**

The verses feature many bright front-vowel sounds. Examples include:

- **EE:** wheels, weary, city, lead, feel, winner
- **IH:** in, this, city, frightened, winner
- **EH:** set, pressure, desperation, let
- **AY:** take, sail, rising, like, lives, eyes, name, slide, pain
- **AE:** and, can, trapped

The repeated AY sound creates a bright, projecting vocal shape and is especially prominent in the chorus.

### Back Vowels

**AH, UH, AO, OE, AW, OO, OW**

The chorus contains many broad and open back-vowel sounds. Examples include:

- **AH:** the, pressure, someone, comes
- **UH:** full
- **AO:** storm, called, story, glory
- **OE:** motion, grown, hold, lonely
- **AW:** down, around, town
- **OO:** you, loser
- **OW:** watch, across, on, gone

The AH and AW sounds in *pressure, down, around* and *town* give the chorus its wide, powerful rock-vocal quality.

### Overall Vocal Pattern

The tighter front vowels carry much of the lyrical detail and forward energy. The broader back vowels provide weight, openness and emotional release — particularly in the chorus.

The strongest recurring vowel colours are: **AY — AH — AW — IH — EE**

For sustained notes, keep the main vowel open and stable before adding the final consonant. For example:

- **down:** sustain AW, then close gently onto *n*
- **take:** sustain AY, then add *k* at the end
- **feel:** sustain EE, then finish with *l*
- **storm:** sustain AO, then complete *rm*

## PART SIX: WHAT THE SONG DEMANDS — RANGE AND REGISTERS

*Measured by the vox-analysis engine across Aaron's nine takes of this song
(seven studio, one phone capture, one live at the Captain Cook Tavern).
Time-weighted note histograms from the strongest studio take.*

### The measured range, as sung

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Working core of the performance | **F♯3 – A♯4** |
| Extremes touched | E3 – A♯5 (top notes are brief ad-lib flicks) |
| Most-lived-in notes | **A♯4** (~14 s), then **C♯4**, **F♯4**, **G♯4**, **D♯4** |
| Verse territory | F♯3 – C♯4, conversational |
| Chorus tessitura | **D♯4 – A♯4**, hook sustains riding F♯4–A♯4 |
| Gear change (estimated passaggio) | **~C♯4–F4** depending on the take; the coaching blueprint pins the trouble spot at the **C♯4 transition** |

### Why this song fits — and where it still bites

Unlike *You're the Voice*, whose chorus camps above the working core, this
song's measured range **matches Aaron's core (G3–A♯4) almost exactly**. That
is why it is a repertoire cornerstone: sing it in the original key; no
transposition question.

The bite is not altitude — it is that the two most-lived notes, **C♯4 and
D4, sit directly on the gear change**. The measured take data (Part Seven)
shows the pitch faults clustering exactly there: five of the eight worst
events in the tavern take were on D4/C♯4, and three of those landed on the
hook line "take the pressure down" itself. The song makes you *live* on the
seam between registers, at conversational pitch, where it is tempting to push
chest weight instead of letting the mix carry it.

Working prescription: treat C♯4–D4 as *mix* territory even though it feels
low enough to muscle. The chorus should feel like lifted speech, not a shout.
Technique back-up:
`aaroncodex/vocal-knowledge-base/03-technique-deep-dives/mixed-voice-development-exercises.md`,
`smooth-vocal-register-transitions.md`, `vocal-register-training-and-blending.md`.

### Style targets

- **Vibrato:** the takes show vibrato on 30–40% of sustained notes at ~4.6–4.9
  Hz — on the slow side. Where it appears (*storm*, held *down*s), let it
  settle within the first half-second rather than arriving late on a straining
  note.
- **Onsets:** the measured signature early in the song is sliding **up** into
  notes (scooping) — the blueprint rule applies verbatim: *land the note
  first, keep the ribs wide, then let the colour arrive.* Farnham's own style
  has bite and swagger, but the pitch centre comes first.
- **Dynamics:** verses conversational, choruses committed — the arrangement's
  contrast is the drama. Spending verse energy at chorus level flattens the
  song and costs the tank you need from the bridge onward.

## PART SEVEN: BREATH CONTROL MAP

*Phrase data measured from the takes: typically 45–61 phrases, median
2.2–2.8 s, with the longest chained chorus passages at 10–14 s. The phrases
are short — this is not a long-line song. The breath story is about what
happens on the **held hook notes** and in the **second half**.*

### What the measurements actually found

The Captain Cook Tavern take got a full engine breath diagnosis (it is what
the drill sheet was built from). The measured pattern:

- **Notes arrive on pitch, hold about a second, then slide down. Sliding,
  not breaking.** That is an air-management fault, not a range fault.
- **The song has two halves.** Early phrases slide *up* into notes
  (scooping); from roughly **2:44 onward** — bridge into final chorus — the
  endings sag *down*, with the worst measured slides at 2:44 (−1.9
  semitones), 3:01 (−2.2) and 3:25 (−0.8). That split by time is the stamina
  story.
- **The faults cluster on the seam.** Five of the eight worst events sat on
  D4/C♯4 — right on the gear change — and three landed on "take the pressure
  down".

One honesty note (from the repo's sag-diagnosis work): a *short* falling
phrase-end is often deliberate rock style, and this song's hook literally
falls on "down" — so not every measured "sag" is a fault. The fault is
specifically the **held note that drifts flat before its release**. Judge by
ear: a chosen fall happens *after* a level hold and sounds the same every
chorus; a leak drifts during the hold and gets bigger as the song goes on.

### Where the breaths go

- **Verses** — breathe at the line breaks; phrases are 2–4 s and low. Easy by
  design. Stay relaxed; bank nothing.
- **Chorus** — one full rib-wide breath **before every line**, every time:
  - breath → "Take the pressure down" (the whole line on one tank, held level)
  - breath → "'Cause I can feel it" — *small top-up after "it"* → "it's rising
    like a storm" (sustain on *storm* needs real air)
  - breath → "Take hold of the wheels and turn them around" — this is the
    long one; if needed, top up after "wheels", never after "turn"
  - breath → "Take the pressure down" — the last "down" of each chorus is the
    one the measurements flag: arrive with reserves
- **Bridge** — the 2:44+ danger zone starts here. Mark: breath before
  "Someone turned…", breath before "I called your name…", and in the long
  line breath after "prisoner": "And I was trapped like a prisoner // in this
  lonely town" — *town* is a held AW and must be fuelled.
- **Verse 3** ("the winner tells his story") — deliberately conversational.
  This is the recovery lap before the final chorus: drop right down, breathe
  low and easy, and arrive at the last chorus with a full tank.

### Breath training for this song specifically

The drill sheet `aaroncodex/docs/practice/pressure-down-breath-support.pdf`
was generated from this exact diagnosis. Its core protocol:

1. **Straw phonation in water** — gentle bubbles, ribs wide. **Count
   steady-bubble seconds, not total seconds:** the rep ends the moment the
   bubbles go uneven or the throat takes over. Chasing maximum duration
   rehearses the sag (squeezing out the last air with collapsing ribs) — the
   exact fault being removed. Log the steady seconds each session.
2. **Ribs stay wide through the release.** Hand on the lower ribs during
   Practice Set 6 (*down* sustains): the ribs must still be wide when the *n*
   closes. If they have collapsed by the release, the breath ended before the
   note did.
3. **Transfer rule (verbatim from the sheet): apply the sensation to one
   short phrase only — do not run the whole song until the cue holds.** One
   "take the pressure down" with a level hold beats ten full runs that
   rehearse the drift.
4. **Sag audit:** record the bridge → final chorus section only. Listen to
   the last second of each held note. Any hold that drifts gets its
   preceding breath enlarged, or the phrase gets a marked top-up. (For a
   measured audit, run the take through the aaroncodex engine — its breath
   section reports exactly these phrase-end slides in cents.)

## PART EIGHT: SPECIFIC PREPARATION PLAN

This song is already in the repertoire and fits the voice — preparation is
about **converting it from "goes well early" to "holds together to the last
chorus"**. Two-week shape, sessions ~25–30 minutes.

### Days 1–3 — Rebuild the hook from silence

- Stages 1–2 (hum + straw). Log steady-bubble seconds.
- Practice Sets 5 and 6 only, at low volume: land–hold–release on *take* and
  *down*. Every hold level, ribs wide to the release.
- One chorus, 70% volume, marked breaths. Stop there — do not run the song.

### Days 4–7 — Own the seam (C♯4–D4)

- Sets 1–4 as vowel groundwork, then mixed-voice sirens through B3–F4
  (`mixed-voice-development-exercises.md`): the goal is that C♯4/D4 feel like
  lifted speech, not the bottom of a shout.
- Sets 7–9 in song rhythm, vowels only, then with light consonants.
- Sag audit on one recorded chorus per session (last second of each hold).

### Days 8–11 — The second half is the song

- Rehearse **bridge → verse 3 → final chorus as a unit**, starting fresh —
  then again *after* singing the first half at performance energy. The gap
  between those two runs is the stamina debt; watch it shrink across sessions.
- Set 10 (bridge lines) and Set 11 (the fast "trapped like a prisoner" line)
  daily — the bridge is where the measured sags began (2:44+).
- Verse 3 practised at deliberately conversational level: it is the recovery
  lap; rehearse the *restraint*, not just the notes.

### Days 12–14 — Full runs

- Two full runs per session: first at 80%, second at performance energy.
  Every marked breath kept, especially the final chorus.
- Record the second run; audit only the held *down*s and *town*. Level holds
  with a chosen, consistent release = performance-ready.

### Performance-night notes (live/karaoke)

- Original key. No transposition decision needed — spend that attention on
  breath marks instead.
- The verses are storytelling: mic close, effort low. The chorus carries
  itself at D♯4–A♯4 — commit, but off the throat.
- The last "take the pressure down" of the night is the one the measurements
  say will drift: it gets the biggest breath you take all song.
- If the tank runs low by the final chorus, shorten the *down* holds and
  release them cleanly and early rather than holding long and drifting flat.
  A short, centred note reads as intent; a long, sagging one reads as tired.

## FINAL SINGING REMINDER

🎤 Let the vowel carry the note.
🎤 Let the consonant briefly shape the word.
🎤 Keep the breath moving through every vowel change.
🎤 Land the note. Hold it level. Then release — fall only by choice.
🎤 Ribs wide to the very end of every *down*.
🎤 Power should come from coordinated breath and resonance, not throat pressure.
